I'm a masters student in Barcelona, studying Artificial Intelligence, so you can imagine that Large Language Models (LLMs) are not just a topic over tapas, but a core part of the toolset for me and my peers.

Who could blame us? One great expectation of LLMs is the democratization of private instruction, allowing us to ask burning questions after class (or *in class!*), or to probe for confirmation of our understanding, or to generate some additional practice problems ahead of the exam.

When it comes to assignments, some students don't even read the prompt first, just gobbling the the pdf into a highlight and dropping it into the chat with a humble *"Please help"*. Others bravely forge forward, trying to avoid turning to the robotic assistants until they encounter a proper challenge.

I've even seen students open a new chat with *"Hello"*. When I asked her why she wasted the tokens on this, she said it makes the responses nicer.
### What kind of prompter are you?

Whether we know it or not, and whether we admit it or not, how we use LLMs says something about our skills, our values, and our desires. Where we spend our time is what we give our attention is what we remember is what we learn. If we prompt away something, it's a declaration that we don't care about it and we won't learn it. Sometimes, that correctly reflects our values. 

### What is worth your time?

- Is reading the assignment worth your time? *If not, ask ChatGPT!*
- Is filling the lab notebook worth your time? *No? Ask away!*
- How about making a study sheet? *ChatGPT is happy to help!*
- And now the exam can be taken from home? *Well...*

In truth, summarizing is one of the things LLMs do best, and they rarely make mistakes on that task. But shortening a document necessarily removes some information.

The labs are our opportunity to practice, but if we don't care about this particular class, or we feel we already understand it, the temptation to speedrun this week's is pretty great.

But when it comes time to study for an exam, that much-loathed and venerable date, the only task is to know the content and show that we know it. It's not busy work and it's not off-topic -- it *IS* the topic. Studying in the age of LLMs can feel like walking between cities when you know there's a bus route. It's not always clear what the extra effort is for, when the power of an LLM is available before and after the exam. But this provokes the **big question**. 

### Why study at all?

As not just knowledge, but actionable, deployable skills have become available with the ease of a prompt, it is growing less and less clear what the purpose of studying is. Surely it's not just a degree to add to the CV, but an opportunity to learn and grow and meet like-minded people.

More than this, I'm a believer that there will still be a place for the craftsmen and craftswomen of the world, even when we have thinking machines at our disposal, and that those who can connect problems with solutions without the automation of an LLM will be valuable.

### Stay in school, kids

For my part, I am embracing the tools at my disposal, but trying to bear in mind that there is a time and place for shortcuts. By skipping steps, I am making a conscious decision to decide that I don't want to give it my attention and therefore I won't learn it. I do this with things like python syntax for plotting and code refactors -- sure, I could do these, but it's not something I want to get good at, and LLMs are amazing for these.

I also use them to check my understanding of lectures, but only after I've had a chance to read a slide or a page myself and I can ask if I've understood correctly. The process of not knowing then learning the answer, and discovering why my first guess was wrong is core to my learning process, and that kind of struggling is interesting and valuable. Accepting the wrong answer or waiting for an email back from the professor does not help my learning process.

I think LLMs will continue to be a growing part of my workflow, and I'm making an effort to get better at working with them in ways that are deliberate and good for me in the long run.

How are you using these powerful new tools.
